## Additional close prior art — Rubinstein, Smith, Barbulescu (AAAI 2012)

Source: https://www.cmu.edu/traffic21/pdfs/incremental-management-of-oversubscribed-vehicle-schedules-in-dynamic-dial-a-ride-problems.pdf
Read all seven pages of extracted text via publisher-hosted author PDF, including Algorithms 1/2, evaluation and references. Figures/layout not visually audited. Generalized Task Swap uses incremental retraction/reinsertion, STN feasibility and a protected set to prevent repeat movement within one invocation. It seeks schedule stability; when insertion/repair fail, the application relaxes the new request's latest pickup/drop windows to serve all demand. This is close prior art against any generic stability-plus-recovery claim. Its described method does not present a lifetime per-rider cumulative absolute ETA revision ledger, the v/e/p accounting split, or episode calibration of exogenous projection variation. Those differences are a bounded comparison to this paper, not evidence of worldwide novelty. Transfer lesson: matched insertion procedures, explicit repair/relaxation stages, preserve which request's contract is relaxed. Do not copy GTS and rename it as RideBound novelty.
