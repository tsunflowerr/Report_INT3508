# ML pilot review: a bounded offline test of policy-conditioned promise residuals

2026-10-08. Specification to seal before fitting; no ML training or dispatch jobs performed in this round.

**Worth testing, but H-ML is not a novel general claim.** Its wording conflicts with its predictions: it says commitment makes error learnable, then predicts that learning helps the uncapped policies while adding little under C-30. A small residual tail can reduce the opportunity for improvement without making the residual more predictable. Rewrite the hypothesis: **quote-time trip and fleet features may improve the reliability/quote-cost frontier within a frozen policy; controlling later dispatch changes may still achieve a better frontier after those predictors are fitted.** This separates the value of learning from the value of operational control.

Ding et al. already learn realized pooled offers from initial request/fleet state, and Leonardi et al. already study simple-input DRT prediction together with operational constraints and fleet size. See the independently checked [pooled-model evidence](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/SURVEY-ML-ETA-CODEX.md:20), [simple-input/constraint evidence](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/SURVEY-ML-ETA-CODEX.md:26), and their linked primary papers. The narrower contribution is an audited cumulative dispatch budget, a frozen historical-trip predictor, and signed residual accounting at measured quote and service costs. This is an empirical combination to investigate, not proof of global novelty.

## What the saved data support

The labels are **simulated** passenger-alight times, even in runs driven by measured citywide traffic factors. They are not observed taxi journey times or real pooled-customer outcomes. Historical profiles and trip-trained predictors are compatible approaches, not alternatives. Static travel also does not make error exactly pure dispatch: the independently verified decomposition is A−P0=Σc+Σx+z, including reprojection drift ([Round 9:15](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/ROUND9-CODEX.md:15)). C-30 allows up to 30 seconds of cumulative dispatch movement when compliant; it does not remove dispatch error or bound traffic/execution error.

**Features and target.** Let D0 be the frozen forecast display, or P0 in static runs; use the single target y=(A−D0)/1000. Base predictors are quote clock-minute (source window-start plus relative t0), P0−t0, pickup−t0, D0−P0, maxRide and room=pickup+maxRide−P0, with all durations in seconds. Do not call maxRide/1.5 observed direct travel time; maxRide is a derived service constraint. Exclude date, request/vehicle IDs and day-type indicators unsupported by weekday-only fitting. No original taxi drop timestamp, final promise/route, realized sharing, future arrivals, revision count, Σc/Σx, exposure or completion status enters predictors. Those are labels, audit variables or outcomes.

The primary state model adds other onboard-rider count, other accepted-but-not-onboard-rider count and stops before the target drop, from the source-ordered initial snapshot/publication. Exclude the target rider. Require reconstruction for every primary eligible row and verify against publication vehicle/epoch and applied plan; missing state is an extraction failure, not zero load. The base-feature model is a prespecified ablation, not an outcome-selected fallback. Publish missingness and all promised-but-unfinished/cancelled counts; error targets are conditional on completion. Report service coverage and timely completions per all requests, plus the common-completed-rider subset across arms. The latter changes the estimand and does not establish a causal policy effect.

**Fixed learners.** Use sklearn 1.7.2 in ml-v1 only. Primary: HistGradientBoostingRegressor(loss=quantile, quantile=0.90, learning_rate=0.05, max_iter=100, max_leaf_nodes=7, min_samples_leaf=20, l2_regularization=1, random_state=20261008, early_stopping=False). Fit the identical model with base features as the ablation. Control: training-standardized linear QuantileRegressor(quantile=0.90, alpha=1, solver=highs), full features. No quantile/hyperparameter search or choosing a winner on test results. Seven arm/world contexts × three learners = **21 offline fits**, zero new dispatch jobs. Before any fitting, write and hash an exclusive input manifest with exact run paths/hashes, source-trip groups, feature schema, profile vintage, split membership, estimator parameters, metric conventions and output names; no fallback substitutions.

**Quote-cost comparison.** For each arm, the quote-cost population is all eligible first-published promises in its sealed runs, one per request, including riders later unfinished or cancelled; it is not selected by future completion. Budgets E∈{15,30,60} seconds are total mean extension from P0 in that population. Define nonnegative score w(x)=max(0,predicted residual), δ=D0−P0. On calibration features only, with mean δ and mean w computed over **all calibration first promises**, set ρ=(E−mean δ)/mean w and F=D0+ρw. If E<mean δ, mark this forecast-based arm/budget infeasible; if mean w=0, use uniform extra padding and label the degenerate model. Controls are P0+E and D0+(E−mean δ), with the same infeasibility rule. P0 and raw D0 are unmatched diagnostic points. Freeze all transforms, ρ and controls before accessing test labels. Never choose the quantile to force mean cost: after clipping/rescaling this is a cost-allocation predictor, not a calibrated 90th-percentile promise.

Calibration-matched cost need not remain matched on a new day. For residual A−F in seconds, Y60 counts strictly greater than +60, early60 counts strictly less than −60, and symmetric accuracy counts the inclusive interval [−60,+60]; all three rates use completed riders. Report actual test mean/p90 extension and mean final quote horizon over **all test first promises**, including later unfinished/cancelled riders; p90 uses nearest rank, ceiling(0.90n). Report completed-cohort and common-completed-cohort quote costs as supplemental. Also report the completed-rider error rates, residual MAE and exact counts. A claimed improvement requires whole-policy, all-promised mean test extension no more than control+1 second, whole-policy p90 no more than control+15 seconds, and completed-rider early60 no more than control+1 percentage point. Otherwise report a cost-frontier point, not a matched-cost win. No test-cohort rescaling or selection of budget/learner is allowed.

## Corrected predictions and stopping

At the primary E=30-second budget, declare the following directional hypotheses before fitting; other budgets are diagnostics. Report all outcomes, not only passes.

| ID | Sealed hypothesis and interpretation |
|---|---|
| ML1 | C-30 measured-factor state-model improvement over frozen forecast+uniform is less than 1 pp on each primary test day, under the cost/early guardrails. This tests the opportunity for gain; it is not equivalence or proof that learning adds nothing. |
| ML2 | U state-model Y60 is at least 1 pp lower than **both** uniform controls on both primary test days, with the guardrails. Compare base/linear results separately to determine whether the extra state features help. |
| ML3 | Static FREE state-model Y60 is at least 3 pp lower than uniform padding on day 13, with positive improvement in each window and the guardrails. The old 16.82% aggregate is context, not this test's baseline. |
| ML4 | Within each world, none of the three prespecified learners for FREE/LOCKS (static) or U (measured-factor) jointly matches C-30's Y60 and symmetric accuracy at feasible E=30 under the cost/early guards. Compare against C-30 in that same world: both w07 test dates for measured-factor simulations, and day 13 for static simulations, using own completed populations and the common-completed-rider diagnostic. Report coverage and quote-horizon differences. This concerns this finite learner family and panel, not all possible ML. |

The w07 test has only 145/200 U and 147/197 C-30 completions on its two dates, from the independent cache. A single C-30 late rider changes a day's rate by about 0.68/0.51 pp; the draft's 0.3 pp claim cannot support statistical equivalence. Samples/windows are correlated, and two held-out dates are not hundreds of independent days. Report exact late/early counts and per-cell/day effects; do not manufacture day-level precision from rider bootstrap or assert conformal coverage under unverified exchangeability ([coverage assumptions:34](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/SURVEY-ML-ETA-CODEX.md:34)). Outcomes were previously inspected, so this is a sealed retrospective pilot, not a fresh prospective holdout.

Stop before fitting for missing/mismatched provenance, world/runner/schema mismatch, duplicate leakage or unreconstructible initial state. Require at least 200 completed fitting trips, 50 first-published calibration promises and 100 completed trips per primary test date (or per static test window), per arm; otherwise label that context insufficient and do not score its hypothesis. Audit cumulative budgets and recovery overrides rather than deleting inconvenient cap breaches. If ML2 and ML3 both fail, stop expanding this learner family and report **no demonstrated gain for this pilot's worlds, inputs and cost budgets**. Do not conclude that historical-trip learning adds nothing, that the hypothesis is globally false, or that simulation gains bound real-world gains. An incident-only failure indicates shift sensitivity; it does not authorize changing the split or retraining on that incident.

The signed ledger can explain errors afterward: A−D0=Σc+(Σx−(D0−P0))+z. A compliant budget bounds the first component. Adding that budget to an empirically calibrated upper forecast for the remaining residual is a specific future hypothesis; this pilot does not claim such a guarantee or feed predicted displays into dispatch.

## Direct answer to the owner

No, the project is not out of useful work. It already has a defensible commitment–service trade-off and signed accounting result, and saved completed trips permit one bounded offline test of whether a simple predictor can improve promises at the same quote cost. Historical-trip ETA learning, pooled fleet-state prediction and operating constraints for predictability already exist, so the thesis should not advertise those as inventions. The useful remaining question is how much prediction compensates for dispatch freedom in this system, and what service/quote costs remain when commitment supplies control. A negative pilot is informative, while the simplified simulator, few dates and simulated labels keep the conclusion limited to this evidence.
