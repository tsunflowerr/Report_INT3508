## 3. Critique of I1–I4 and the cap/pad reading

### I1 — room-based padding: promising feature, qualified evidence

**Room is available at the initial promise and is a valid candidate feature.** It is initial scheduled onboard slack, `r=P_pick,0+T_max−P_drop,0`. From the ride-time constraint,

`p_drop−P_drop,0 ≤ r + (p_pick−P_pick,0)`.

Thus fixed dispatch-induced pickup times do not make r a hard bound on total drop drift: exogenous pickup drift still matters. Our reproduction finds **zero pre-boarding pickup dispatch exposure**, but exogenous pickup exposure for **2,356 U and 2,262 C-30 riders** ([output:22–23](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round5-reproduction-2026-10-07.txt:22)). The bounded formula limits the added reserve, not forecast extension plus reserve.

Room is also confounded with journey length and forecast exposure. Across U's extreme room buckets, mean initial drop horizon rises from **685.2 to 1,131.6 s**, and mean allowed ride time from **315.6 to 1,096.9 s** ([output:32–35](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round5-reproduction-2026-10-07.txt:32)). Compare room against horizon, allowed-ride-time/direct-time and calendar baselines, using identical split and parameter-search effort. The bucket association alone does not establish an independent risk signal or a causal pooling effect.

There is stronger **in-sample** evidence than Claude's unequal-cost comparison: at the room rule's exact mean extension, uniform padding yields **74 late riders / 2,360 timely**, versus **48 / 2,386** for the room rule ([output:18](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round5-reproduction-2026-10-07.txt:18), [output:14](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round5-reproduction-2026-10-07.txt:14)). However, p90 extension is **171.2 s** for room versus **107.1 s** for the matched-mean uniform rule. Room improves late/early counts at this mean cost while increasing tail quote inflation; it does not dominate every promise-cost dimension.

Accept chronological fitting. Training on 14 November and evaluating 15/16 November is a chronological **backtest**, with an incident day separately reported. These outcomes were already inspected while developing the rule, so it is not a fresh holdout. Leave-one-day-out fitting can train on future outcomes; use it only as retrospective robustness. A claim based on five independent test days cannot follow from the weekday split alone.

### The crossover — supported as a sample frontier, not a universal optimum

The arithmetic is sound, but the slogan at [IDEAS-CLAUDE:88](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/claude-work/IDEAS-CLAUDE.md:88) is too precise. Independently reproduced U proportional padding yields **2,342 timely at 50.0 s mean extension**, already above C-30's **2,338 served ceiling** ([output:19](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round5-reproduction-2026-10-07.txt:19), [output:3](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round5-reproduction-2026-10-07.txt:3)). Our exploratory cost-matched envelope gives C/U **2,336/2,324 at E=30 s**, **2,338/2,335 at E=40 s**, and **2,338/2,342 at E=50 s** ([output:42–47](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round5-reproduction-2026-10-07.txt:42)). This is an in-sample envelope over the specified families, not a universal crossover or a fitted deployment policy.

For fixed A, `1{A−D≤τ}` can only improve when D increases. This is a legitimate **reference-relative fulfillment** metric, but it can be optimized by loosening the reference; it does not show faster transport or increased welfare. For the two required U policies, two-sided accuracy `|A−D|≤60 s` gives only **89 versus 1,041 riders**, although both have **2,386 timely** ([output:13–14](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round5-reproduction-2026-10-07.txt:13)). Report tolerance sensitivity, early/late error, p90 extension, full initial quote horizon, actual journey outcomes and served-set composition. Equal added extension is not necessarily equal total quote length across arms. Without booking responses and rider costs, no business-optimal E or welfare winner is identified.

### I2 — narrow full-panel cap sweep: agree, revise predictions

I accept calibration first and the full existing panel for estimating small service differences; my six-cell proposal should serve as an execution/anchoring pilot. I also accept deferring a large matched-M grid until there is a specific contract-comparison hypothesis, while withholding any claim that C beats M where M was not run.

Remove monotonic-service predictions: the development report already shows **666 completions at C-120 versus 665 at C-240** ([W07X:25,28](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/tang3/w07x/W07X-REPORT.md:25)). Failure of one cap does not kill tuning if another cap provides a useful frontier. Seal candidate-specific predictions and retire the lever only when the tested range has no useful nondominated point. Job arithmetic is checkable; extrapolated elapsed hours need a measured scheduling basis and are not guaranteed.

### I3 — density before fleet-only tuning: agree conditionally

Scale demand and fleet together before changing fleet alone if fixed geometry, nested demand, comparable trip composition and nested vehicle placement can be preserved. Regenerating the normalizer's selected node pool may change compatibility and trip lengths as well as density. Increasing caps or accepting harder demand can also change who is completed. A density pilot then measures a controlled scale sensitivity, not proof that scale alone caused the original sharing rate. A default build in `C:/Code/RideBound-noworse` would violate the current write boundary; use a prebuilt executable or keep build artifacts inside codex-work.

**A concrete feasibility gap:** the current normalizer requires **RequestTarget≤128, VehicleCount≤32 and MaximumNodeCount≤96** ([preflight:326–328](E:/Code/RideBound-deadline/src/RideBound.Benchmarking/Normalization/FleetPyManhattanNormalizer.cs:326)). Claude's proposed **270/20** and **540/40** fixtures cannot be generated unchanged. The density pilot needs an audited scaffold extension and checks of downstream limits; its job arithmetic does not include that development. A prebuilt anchor regeneration is plausible, but the CLI writes beneath its `--repository` root ([Program.cs:91–103](E:/Code/RideBound-deadline/tools/RideBound.Wp6Normalize/Program.cs:91)); use a staged root and writable cache inside codex-work rather than pointing it at an external repository.

For anchor sensitivity, preserve `MaximumRideTimePermille=1500` and recompute factor-derived direct times and `maxRideTimeMs` consistently; audit unchanged request identities/times, nodes and vehicle placements ([normalizer:427–475](E:/Code/RideBound-deadline/src/RideBound.Benchmarking/Normalization/FleetPyManhattanNormalizer.cs:427)). Freezing the old numeric ride allowance while changing edge times would change the intended structural rule and can make a direct ride infeasible.

### I4 — retain an accounting KPI, change the responsibility labels

With forecast extension `b_fc` and deliberate reserve `q`, use the signed identity

`A−P0 = b_fc + q + Σc + R`, with `R=A−F0−Σc` and `F0=P0+b_fc+q`.

Keep `Σ|c|` as the contract cost, separately from signed `Σc`. R may be negative and includes forecast/execution/model error; it is not automatically unforeseeable traffic. Forecast extension is an allowance in a quote, not operator-caused lateness. Report raw experienced lateness without exclusions, and any explicit incident-based responsibility measure alongside it. CFR is an analogy for this distinction, not a prescription for this ledger.

### Hybrid scope — defer, without claiming a counterfactual proof

Claude's rider-level screen also reproduces. At C-30/S=60, **79 riders hit the deadline and 75 have no dispatch component** ([output:58](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round5-reproduction-2026-10-07.txt:58)). Our revision-level screen finds only **two chosen new violations**, `e≤P0+S<p`, versus **2,712 reference-and-candidate failures**; at S=120 it finds **zero new violations and 431 joint failures** ([output:58–59](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round5-reproduction-2026-10-07.txt:58)). These counts strengthen the case for deferring the C-30 hybrid.

They do not prove a hybrid would add no reliability: saved chosen publications omit alternative plans, rejected requests and changed downstream routes. Screen a looser C history for newly induced violations, rescues and recorded reference failures before proposing hybrid runs. Retire my immediate hybrid pilot from the joint plan.

## 4. What remains missing

The joint work still needs a frozen chronological calibration comparison against simpler risk features, consistent per-cell/per-day weighting, common-rider and whole-policy reporting, a clearly costed cap sweep, an anchor decision, documented dwell assumptions, and an incident policy that does not define excuses by forecast error. A fresh holdout would be needed for a general predictive claim; more episodes from the same few days do not supply it. Implementing a live forecast-visible certified ledger is additional work and is outside the proposed five-week core. The next plan should deliver one audited frontier and explicit negative findings before adding mechanisms.
