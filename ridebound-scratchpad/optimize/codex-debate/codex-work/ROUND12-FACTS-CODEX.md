The published 1.4% is not specifically the probability of extra delay.

The corresponding [U arithmetic](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round12-evidence-audit.txt:6) is 2,434 completed, 174 exposed riders (7.149%), 187 nonzero events, median absolute magnitude 108.636 s. Existing detours in the first accepted plan are already included in the promise. These counts measure later dispatch changes, including changes before boarding; they do not count all sharing detours against a direct private trip, establish that every change was a new insertion, or describe fares.

The [budget diagnostic](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/claude-work/budget-block-diagnosis-2026-10-07.txt:2) reports 32 also rejected by U plus 116 served by U = 148 C rejections, with median minimum reported overshoot 109.6 s. Only 2/148 witnesses had prior budget spending ([source](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/claude-work/budget-block-diagnosis-2026-10-07.txt:10)); these are mostly large immediate proposed changes.

## F2. Q3: first-promise computation and the meaning of low lateness

The projected schedule starts at evaluation time and the actual vehicle node/edge-progress position ([initial state](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Application/Scheduling/RouteScheduleProjector.cs:62)). It walks remaining stops in order, looks up each leg in one current travel-time snapshot, computes arrival, service start and departure, then advances to the stop node ([stop loop](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Application/Scheduling/RouteScheduleProjector.cs:105)). It does not query a different future-departure travel-time model for each leg. Pickups use max(arrival, earliestPickup); other stops use arrival ([GetServiceStart](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Application/Scheduling/RouteScheduleProjector.cs:151)).

The promise stores projected pickup and drop service starts ([pickup](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Application/Promises/PromiseProjector.cs:79), [drop](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Application/Promises/PromiseProjector.cs:131)). The first publication is booking confirmation in these configurations ([publication setting](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/tang3/configs/wp4-tier3-v2.json:5)), not necessarily request arrival. An already-boarded request retains its earlier pickup promise or recovers actual pickup from the executed prefix ([onboard exceptions](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Application/Promises/PromiseProjector.cs:81)).

The generic projector adds stop.ServiceDuration ([duration support](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Application/Scheduling/RouteScheduleProjector.cs:125)); therefore "duration is always zero" is false as a core-code claim. These candidates set pickup and drop durations to zero ([pickup](E:/Code/RideBound-deadline/src/RideBound.Algorithms/Candidates/InsertionCandidateGenerator.cs:525), [drop](E:/Code/RideBound-deadline/src/RideBound.Algorithms/Candidates/InsertionCandidateGenerator.cs:531)), and the adapter sets constant boarding time to zero ([FleetPy config](E:/Code/RideBound-deadline/simulators/fleetpy-ridebound/actual_fleetpy_medium_preflight.py:241)). The adapter passes protocol stop duration to FleetPy ([stop conversion](E:/Code/RideBound-deadline/simulators/fleetpy-ridebound/ridebound_fleetpy/fleet_control.py:948)).

For this measured-factor panel, the world uses real-derived traffic profiles with sigma=0 and shockRate=0 ([runner description](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/tang3/t38/run_t38.py:8), [world configuration](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/tang3/calib/panelA/worlds/W5-d20181115-w07.json:1)). Execution is deterministic conditional on the chosen scenario/seed and dispatch sequence. The runner sees flat current-condition snapshots while execution can cross later factor bins ([world/runner distinction](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/tang3/world/preflight_world.py:15)); this is coupled simulation, not an independent dataset of actual pooled riders and their noisy arrival labels.

"Only later traffic and later dispatch can cause error" is too exact. Discretization, rounding and execution/projection discrepancies can remain. The signed ledger separates A-P0 = sum(c)+sum(x)+z, rather than assuming z=0. In the static WP14r verification, all 80 runs have one travel update ([update audit](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round9-static-snapshot-verification-2026-10-08.txt:84)), yet 1,037 completed riders have tiny nonzero X in [-997,0] ms, with z=0 ([residual audit](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/round9-ladder-reproduction-2026-10-08.txt:15)). Low lateness combines deterministic execution, current-model projection, service bounds and the served cohort; it is a simulation result, not an empirical universal ETA fact.

## F3. Published rule mapping: component versus full algorithm

**Schulz/Pfeiffer deadline ([S10](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/ROUND12-SOURCES-CODEX-v2.md:59)).** Mplus-30 already implements the fixed offered-drop-anchor plus 30 s component: [config](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/tang3/configs/Mplus-30.json:98) and [deadline comparison](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Domain/Commitments/CommitmentLockEvaluator.cs:186). Changing slack to the paper's 900 s is configuration only. The full potential/reoptimization algorithm needs core work, and traffic recovery exemptions make our enforcement differ from an unconditional deadline guarantee. C-30 has a cumulative absolute movement limit, so it is not this deadline component.

**Horl/Zwick strategy B ([S11](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/ROUND12-SOURCES-CODEX-v2.md:65)).** Existing [no-worse-than-reference recovery](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/tang3/configs/wp4-tier3-v2.json:7) and [commitment exemptions](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Application/Commitments/CommitmentDecisionValidator.cs:413) are related. But physical travel-quality relief is only for the unchanged safety no-op; changed candidates remain strictly contractual ([scope and simulator reason](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Domain/Validation/ServiceQualityAllowance.cs:75), [candidate generation](E:/Code/RideBound-deadline/src/RideBound.Algorithms/Candidates/InsertionCandidateGenerator.cs:161)). Exact B, allowing extra pickups subject to updated onboard drop bounds and penalties, requires physical-bound handling, matching simulator feasibility and objective changes. It is not a configuration toggle.

**Fielbaum/Alonso-Mora controlled scenarios ([S12](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/ROUND12-SOURCES-CODEX-v2.md:71)).** Retaining accepted riders is already common to all arms ([accepted-rider preservation](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Domain/Validation/PhysicalPlanValidator.cs:699)), representing the non-rejection component of the intermediate scenario. C/U/Mplus also fix vehicle and confirmed pickup ETA; they are stricter in some dimensions, not exact reproductions. A pickup ratchet can express no dispatch worsening via config ([lock fields](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Runner/Configuration/CommitmentPolicyConfiguration.cs:20), [ratchet inequality](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Domain/Commitments/CommitmentLockEvaluator.cs:204)). However, active vehicle lock is unconditional ([vehicle lock](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Domain/Commitments/CommitmentLockEvaluator.cs:42)), as is physical incumbent vehicle preservation ([vehicle invariant](C:/Code/RideBound-noworse/src/RideBound.Domain/Validation/PhysicalPlanValidator.cs:682)). Full controlled replication permits reassignment and updates an absolute accepted latest-pickup bound; both need core changes. An exogenous-to-candidate ratchet alone is not that absolute historical bound under changing traffic.

**Alonso-Mora latest-pickup tightening ([S13](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/ROUND12-SOURCES-CODEX-v2.md:77)).** Same partial configuration proxy and same core gaps: fixed/nonincreasing accepted pickup bound with pre-pickup vehicle rematching, then vehicle fixation after boarding. This rule concerns pickup rather than a drop budget.

**Uber arrival assurance ([S14](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/ROUND12-SOURCES-CODEX-v2.md:83)).** A decision-induced C-0 config is a conservative component proxy: zero cumulative absolute drop movement disallows improvements too. A one-sided drop-ETA ratchet is configurable, permitting improvements, but generic recovery can exempt already-violated gates and these are not proprietary optimizer replications. The public product page explicitly allows traffic/other delays and does not disclose its initial ETA padding or enforcement algorithm.

**Additional strong implementable component.** Williams/Hoel live-corrected history ([S2](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/codex-debate/codex-work/ROUND12-SOURCES-CODEX-v2.md:11)) is already structurally represented by [factor ratio](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/promise_stretch.py:9) and [one-sided display](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/display_layer.py:5). Feeding it or damped forecasts into Mplus is a forecast-aware deadline design; changing deadline-based acceptances needs new dispatch trajectories, not a promise-only replay. The saved C-30F/Mplus-30F comparison reports forced-reference decision rates 0%/47.6% on 15 completed cells ([existing comparison](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/FORECAST-REPORT.md:134)), but also three feasibility stops ([integration failures](E:/Code/Report_INT3508/ridebound-scratchpad/optimize/FORECAST-REPORT.md:120)). Do not call that a clean full-paper reproduction.
