# Reading ledger (updated 2026-09-18)

Page numbers below are PDF page numbers, not journal page numbers. All 13 originals are hashed in `corpus-inventory.json`; extracted text alone is not counted as a completed reading. This file records conservative coverage and remains open.

| ID | Source | Coverage actually read | Implication for RideBound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Choice-driven DARP, Sharif Azadeh et al. 2022 | Text pages 1–22 complete; truncated page 4 reread separately | Already offers menu of pickup windows and service types, confirms window first and exact ETA when vehicle departs; freezes active arc, permits rerouting before departure; maximizes empty waiting via LP. Thus staged promise disclosure, window offers, waiting relocation, personalized flexibility and pricing are prior art. Models utility using worst permissible duration, not realized burden. Choice coefficients borrowed; no RideBound satisfaction inference. |
| 02 | Wardman et al. value-of-time multipliers 2026 | All22 text pages read; pages15–16 reread after truncation; image-only tables pages5,6,19 visually inspected | 2,872 multipliers/606 studies; 83% SP, high-income and UK evidence dominate (Vietnam3 multipliers/2 studies). Reliability definitions distinguish SD, schedule delay and systematic lateness. No cumulative ETA revision valuation. SP/RP and time-period effects challenge universal waiting multiplier2. Variable selection and outlier exclusions involve judgment; cannot use the selected meta-model as calibrated Vietnamese promise-disutility. |
| 03 | Phạm Xuân Bách thesis 2024 | Text pages1–65 complete; pages9–12 reread after truncation; algorithm pages25,30,36,42 and result figures47–49 visually inspected | Prophet prepositions vehicles with synthetic predicted requests; Prune Prophet narrows insertion positions and explicitly sacrifices optimality. Prediction input is generated from actual request data, not an independently evaluated forecast. Printed algorithms omit same-position feasibility checks and use a deadline comparison opposite to prose; these are document issues, not verified implementation defects. Figures/text disagree on taxi counts and runtime units require verification. Cannot inherit linear-time optimality, prediction accuracy or speedup as RideBound evidence. |
| 04 | HCRide 2025 | Text pages 1–9 complete | Ride-hailing, active driver cannot take new request until completion; fleet-level expected CMDP cost, not per-rider pathwise commitment budget. Historical visit frequency proxies preference. It supplies neither pooled insertion algorithm nor deterministic individual promise certificate. |
| 05 | Bregni et al. MRP nervousness 2011 | All 4 pages read | Two cover pages plus short study; lot-for-lot decoupling under a narrow production model. Cannot transfer claim of cost-free RideBound decoupling. |
| 06 | Freezing MPS, Sridharan et al. 1987 | Text pages 1–14 complete; formulas have OCR issues, visual inspection pending if used quantitatively | Order vs period freezing and horizon length studied, but future quantities in the rolling window assumed accurate. Approximately 50% freeze result is simulated production/inventory cost, not a universal ridepool threshold. Freezing method comparison also changes rolling procedure. |
| 07 | Kovacs et al. consistency routing survey 2014 | Text pages 1–22 complete; page5 reread after truncation. Two-column extraction interleaves columns, so verify figures/formulas visually before using them quantitatively | Arrival-time, person-oriented and delivery consistency long predate RideBound. Hard/soft consistency, preassigned time windows, route templates and flexible departure times are prior art. Repeated-day consistency differs from within-trip revision. Reported cost increases depend strongly on departure/waiting constraints. |
| 08 | Molenbruch et al. operational service-level effects 2015 | Text pages 3–22 complete; pages 1–2 visually inspected (cover and copyright notice) | 78 combinations of pickup-preference deviation and relative excess ride time. Static deterministic, unlimited available fleet; distance objective with no incentive to minimize user inconvenience. Service-time threshold can make small slack unusable; multi-dimensional slack matters jointly. Cost elasticity diminishes while average inconvenience grows. This motivates testing useful insertion opportunity, but does not prove a new score. |
| 09 | Rauh et al. primary-care DARP 2025 | All27 text pages read; pseudocode22 and MIP24 visually inspected | Mini-clusters exploit advance chronic appointments with flexible scheduling, then insert return rides and walk-in patients. Relaxing time windows alone worsens greedy baseline; clustering is necessary in this study. 38% gain compares both algorithm and service setting changes, not a promise-preserving RideBound improvement. Detour rises9.1%→11.7% in rural setting. Solver baseline has different extra constraints and times out20+ requests, so do not call its incumbent an optimality certificate. Printed AppendixD omits an explicit at-most-once pickup bound; actual code unavailable, so this is a formulation clarification, not a demonstrated implementation defect. |
| 10 | Schulz & Pfeiffer customer-oriented dynamic DARP 2026 | Text pages 1–46 complete; page40 reread after truncation. Algorithms visually checked pages 18,20,21,37; configuration table checked page42 | Strong direct comparator: precomputed remove/reinsert and future potential; changed tour and related precomputed alternatives are excluded from potential during insertion. Sampling/clustering/parallelism already evaluated. Do not claim generic anticipatory selection as new. |
| 11 | Scale analysis on-demand ridepooling | All30 text pages read | Demand scale, pooling, fleet activation and passenger delay jointly affect efficiency. Model admits all requests and activates fleet endogenously; this differs from fixed-fleet acceptance under commitments. Daily unreliability valuation is not within-trip ETA revision valuation. Do not interpret fleet economies or walking as free passenger benefit. |
| 12 | Fielbaum & Alonso-Mora unreliability 2020 | Text pages 1–20 read; page6 reread after truncation | Endpoint increase vs first announcement, increase count rather than all forecast changes. Their fully controlled rule already prevents waiting-time increases. Includes reassignment and withdrawing acceptance in baseline; different from present RideBound generator. |
| 13 | Alonso-González et al. value of time/reliability 2020 | Text all 21 pages; figures/choice tasks on pages4–5 visually inspected | 1,006 Dutch respondents, stated preference with five equally likely durations. VOR concerns across-trip variability and systematic displacement, not the cumulative movement of ETA shown during one trip. Reliability ratio ~0.5 is no calibration for a revision budget; most transfer reliability coefficients insignificant. Waiting-time disutility is nonlinear; service availability also matters. |
| 14 | Rubinstein et al. GTS AAAI 2012 (new download) | Main content all 7 pages read via primary PDF/web and local extract | Conflict-set retraction/reinsertion with protected requests and rollback is prior art. User schedule stability explicitly motivates repair. Public PDF in downloads/. |
| 15 | Sivagnanam et al. prompt confirmation ICCPS 2026, arXiv v2 (new download) | Text pages 1–16 complete | Quick insertion plus anytime simulated annealing; value learning guides both. Accepted riders retain service feasibility, onboard vehicle fixed; waiting requests may reassign. Uses idle-time/spatio-temporal availability features, no cumulative rider ledger. Critical ablation: simple idle heuristic already nearly matches learned variants; no basis to assume adding a neural model pays off. |
