# Citation Information

**Full Citation:**
Burchard, A., Liebeherr, J., & Patek, S. D. (2006). A Calculus for End-to-end Statistical Service Guarantees. *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory*, 52, 4105-4114. (arXiv:cs/0205001)

# Abstract

"The deterministic network calculus offers an elegant framework for determining delays and backlog in a network with deterministic service guarantees to individual traffic flows. This paper addresses the problem of extending the network calculus to a probabilistic framework with statistical service guarantees. Here, the key difficulty relates to expressing, in a statistical setting, an end-to-end (network) service curve as a concatenation of per-node service curves. The notion of an effective service curve is developed as a probabilistic bound on the service received by an individual flow. It is shown that per-node effective service curves can be concatenated to yield a network effective service curve."

# Key Concepts

- **Effective Service Curve:** A probabilistic bound characterizing the service a flow receives
- **Statistical Service Curve:** Per-node probabilistic service guarantees that can be aggregated
- **Statistical Network Service Curve:** The concatenation of per-node effective service curves forming an end-to-end guarantee
- **Statistical Multiplexing Gain:** The paper's framework permits quantifying gains through probabilistic composition rather than deterministic worst-case analysis
- **Admission Control:** The effective service curve methodology enables probabilistic admission control decisions based on statistical guarantees

## Abstract (Verbatim)
"The widespread adoption of digital distribution channels both enables and forces more and more logistical service providers to manage booking processes actively to maintain competitiveness. As a result, their operational planning is no longer limited to solving vehicle routing problems. Instead, demand management decisions and vehicle routing decisions are optimized integratively with the aim of maximizing revenue and minimizing fulfillment cost. The resulting integrated demand management and vehicle routing problems (i-DMVRPs) can be formulated as Markov decision process models and, theoretically, can be solved via the well-known Bellman equation. Unfortunately, the Bellman equation is intractable for realistic-sized instances. Thus, in the literature, i-DMVRPs are often addressed via decomposition-based solution approaches involving an opportunity cost approximation as a key component. Despite its importance, to the best of our knowledge, there is neither a technique to systematically analyze how the accuracy of the opportunity cost approximation translates into overall solution quality nor are there general guidelines on when to apply which class of approximation approach. In this work, we address this research gap by proposing an explainability technique that quantifies and visualizes the magnitude of approximation errors, their immediate impact, and their relevance in specific regions of the state space. Exploiting reward decomposition, it further yields a characterization of different types of approximation errors. Applying the technique to a generic i-DMVRP in a full-factorial computational study and comparing the results with observations in existing literature, we show that the technique contributes to better explaining algorithmic performance and provides guidance for the algorithm selection and development process."

The paper addresses microtransit scheduling under demand uncertainty and heterogeneous time preferences through a Chance-Constrained Dial-A-Ride Problem with Soft Time Windows (CCDARP-STW). The framework accommodates spontaneous spatially-aligned requests and time flexibility via soft time windows with penalties. A bounded-support linear relaxation tackles nonlinear chance constraints, and a Branch-and-Cut-and-Price algorithm with probabilistic dominance rules provides efficient solutions. Real-world Chicago data demonstrates 11.55-minute travel time savings and 96.46% service reliability compared to conventional approaches.

The token-bucket specification TB(r,B) defines rate limiting through two parameters: a token fill rate *r* (tokens/sec) representing average transmission capacity, and bucket capacity *Bmax* representing maximum burstiness. Packets are compliant when the bucket contains sufficient tokens; non-compliant packets may be delayed (shaping), dropped (policing), or marked.

## Multiple-Bucket Specifications

Systems often employ cascading token buckets to enforce both peak and average rates. For instance, one filter might specify a peak rate with small bucket, while another defines average rate over longer intervals with larger bucket. This dual approach prevents sustained bursts from exceeding policy constraints while permitting temporary rate spikes.

## Delay Bounds and Queue Utilization

A critical relationship emerges between token-bucket parameters and queuing delay. The document establishes that:

**"the maximum delay faced by any TB packet is Bmax/r, exactly as for traffic through a single router."**

This bound applies whether traffic passes through one or multiple routers with sufficient output bandwidth. Queue space consumed by compliant traffic is bounded by the bucket capacity itself.

## Parekh-Gallager Theorem

This theorem provides comprehensive delay bounds for token-bucket-constrained flows traversing networks with weighted-fair-queuing guarantees. Total delay encompasses: propagation delays, burst-induced queuing (B/f), store-and-forward delays per router, and fair-queuing scheduling delays. The bound elegantly combines token-bucket specifications with fair-queuing analysis.

## Admission Control Application

Routers employ token-bucket specifications for admission decisions—accepting new connections based on requested rate and bucket requirements. This framework enables capacity planning while maintaining performance guarantees for existing flows.

# Abstract

"Technology of autonomous vehicles (AVs) is getting mature and many AVs will appear on the roads in the near future. AVs become connected with the support of various vehicular communication technologies and they possess high degree of control to respond to instantaneous situations cooperatively with high efficiency and flexibility. In this paper, we propose a new public transportation system based on AVs. It manages a fleet of AVs to accommodate transportation requests, offering point-to-point services with ride sharing. We focus on the two major problems of the system: scheduling and admission control. The former is to configure the most economical schedules and routes for the AVs to satisfy the admissible requests while the latter is to determine the set of admissible requests among all requests to produce maximum profit. The scheduling problem is formulated as a mixed-integer linear program and the admission control problem is cast as a bilevel optimization, which embeds the scheduling problem as the major constraint. By utilizing the analytical properties of the problem, we develop an effective genetic-algorithm-based method to tackle the admission control problem. We validate the performance of the algorithm with real-world transportation service data."
