# Integrated Demand Management and Vehicle Routing: Key Components

## i-DMVRP Formulation

The problem integrates two decisions for each arriving customer request: demand control (accept/reject) and vehicle routing. It's modeled as a Markov Decision Process with state $s_t = (\mathcal{C}_t, \phi_t)$ comprising confirmed orders and tour plans. The objective maximizes expected profit: revenue minus fulfillment costs.

## Reward Decomposition

Opportunity cost decomposes into two components:
- **Displacement Cost (DPC)**: Loss of future revenue when accepting a request
- **Marginal Cost-to-Serve (MCTS)**: Increase in fulfillment costs from acceptance

Mathematically: $\Delta V_t(s_{t-1},c) = \Delta R_t(s_{t-1},c) + \Delta F_t(s_{t-1},c)$

## Taxonomy of Approximation Error Types

**DPC Policy** (neglects MCTS):
- Underestimation Type 1: Neglects better consolidation opportunities for future requests
- Underestimation Type 2: Ignores inherent request unprofitability
- Overestimation: Protects capacity for high-revenue demand with poor consolidation

**MCTS Policy** (neglects DPC):
- Underestimation Type 1: Fails to reserve capacity for future high-revenue demand
- Underestimation Type 2: Missing information about future demand volume
- Overestimation: Wrongly attributes high routing costs to early arrivals

## Opportunity Cost Approximation Definition

The authors compute true OC by solving Bellman equations exactly via backwards recursion. They compare against policies using only one component: 

"DPC-based approximation...neglects fulfillment cost" and "MCTS-based approximation neglects displacement cost."

## Main Empirical Conclusions

The research identifies that underestimation dominates: occurring in 89.4% of DPC settings and 69.7% of MCTS settings. This causes greedy decision-making where providers accept too many requests or underprice services. The findings align with observed behavior across diverse real-world applications in existing literature, demonstrating fundamental validity beyond their simplified test cases.

# Dynamic Programming Model for Optimal Delivery Slot Pricing

## State Definition

**State space:** $x \in X = \{x \in \mathbb{Z}^{\bar{s}} : 0 \leq x_s \leq \bar{x}_s \text{ for all } s \in S\}$

where $x_s$ denotes the number of confirmed orders in delivery slot $s$, with $S = \{1, 2, \ldots, \bar{s}\}$ representing all available slots and $t \in T = \{1, 2, \ldots, \bar{t}\}$ the booking horizon.

## Decision Variables

**Price vector:** $d = [d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_{\bar{s}}]^\intercal$ with $d_s \in [\underline{d}, \bar{d}]$ for each slot $s$, or $d_s = \infty$ (slot not offered).

## Customer Choice Model

The paper employs a multinomial logit specification:

$$\Pi_s(d) := \frac{\exp(\beta_c + \beta_s + \beta_d d_s)}{\sum_{k \in S}\exp(\beta_c + \beta_k + \beta_d d_k) + 1}$$

where $\beta_d < 0$ captures price sensitivity. Customer arrival rate: $p_s(d) := \lambda \Pi_s(d)$ with $\lambda \in (0,1)$.

## Bellman Recursion (Value Function)

$$V_t(x) := \max_{d \in D} \left\{ \sum_s p_s(d)[r + d_s + V_{t+1}(x+1_s) - V_{t+1}(x)] + V_{t+1}(x) \right\}$$

with terminal condition $V_{\bar{t}+1}(x) = -C(x)$ where $C(x)$ approximates vehicle routing costs.

**Compact operator form:** $V_{t-1} := \mathcal{T}V_t$ for all $t \in T$.

## Opportunity Cost (Marginal Value of Capacity)

$$\gamma_{s,t}(x) := V_t(x) - V_t(x + 1_s) \geq 0$$

This represents the expected profit foregone by accepting one additional order in slot $s$ at state $x$ and time $t$.

## Key Structural Results

**Theorem 1 (Infinite Horizon):** Under Assumption 1 (marginal delivery cost bounded by max price plus base revenue), the unique fixed point is:

$$V^*(x) = (\bar{d} + r)\mathbf{1}^\intercal(\bar{x} - x) - C(\bar{x})$$

Interpretation: optimal opportunity cost is constant across slots—charge maximum price $\bar{d}$ to all slots.

**Theorem 2 (Finite Horizon Concavity):** Under Assumptions 1–3 and sufficiently small $\lambda > 0$, the value function $V_t$ is "concave-extensible" (admits continuous concave extension) for all $t \in T$.

**Proposition 4:** Under Assumption 2 (strictly increasing opportunity costs at terminal condition), the opportunity cost itself exhibits "increasing differences" property:
$$\gamma_{s,t}(x + 1_{s'}) > \gamma_{s,t}(x) \quad \text{for } s \neq s'$$

This ensures optimal prices increase monotonically with order congestion.
